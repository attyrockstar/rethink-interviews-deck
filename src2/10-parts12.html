Product sense, tested live

“Why does Zomato show the delivery time before you have picked a dish?”

There is a line, and most answers never cross it. You do not need to be profound for the whole hour. You need it once.

Illustrative answers, not Zomato's stated strategy.

Obvious
So the user knows when the food will arrive.Every candidate says it. It restates the feature.
Interesting
It sets the expectation before the commitment, so fewer people cancel.Now you are reasoning about behaviour, not the screen.
Profound
The time promise is the product. Restaurants are inventory. Whoever owns the promise owns the customer.The answer the interviewer repeats to the panel.

The answer that ends the loop

“I am a business person. Next question, please.”

Engineering usually has a large say in the hiring decision. As a PM, technology is your business. This reads as an unwillingness to learn, and it travels straight back to the panel.

The gate is not what you think

71 of 100 PMs in India have no software degree

Belong analysed 15,000 software PMs in India. Only 29% held a software-engineering degree, and only 6% had the engineering-plus-MBA combination everyone assumes is the ticket. What the rest had was proof they had already done parts of the job.

Right answer: your current company

Do the job before you get the title

PMs are busy and can always use help from a capable colleague. Four moves, starting Monday. Give it six months, then put it at the top of your resume.

1

Offer your PM specific help

“I will run the weekly complaint triage for eight weeks and bring you the top three patterns.” A vague offer is easy to decline.

2

Shadow the core team meetings

You are learning how decisions get made and how disagreement gets resolved. No course teaches that.

3

Become the biggest source of ideas

For the product you already work on. Then implement them. Shipped ideas make you hard to ignore.

4

Take a good PM class

Mostly for the vocabulary you will be expected to speak fluently in interviews.
