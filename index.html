Product sense, tested live

“Why does Zomato show the delivery time before you have picked a dish?”

There is a line, and most answers never cross it. You do not need to be profound for the whole hour. You need it once.

Illustrative answers, not Zomato's stated strategy.

Obvious
So the user knows when the food will arrive.Every candidate says it. It restates the feature.
Interesting
It sets the expectation before the commitment, so fewer people cancel.Now you are reasoning about behaviour, not the screen.
Profound
The time promise is the product. Restaurants are inventory. Whoever owns the promise owns the customer.The answer the interviewer repeats to the panel.

The answer that ends the loop

“I am a business person. Next question, please.”

Engineering usually has a large say in the hiring decision. As a PM, technology is your business. This reads as an unwillingness to learn, and it travels straight back to the panel.

Right answer: your current company

Do the job before you get the title

PMs are busy and can always use help from a capable colleague. Four moves, starting Monday. Give it six months, then put it at the top of your resume.

1

Offer your PM specific help

“I will run the weekly complaint triage for eight weeks and bring you the top three patterns.” A vague offer is easy to decline.

2

Shadow the core team meetings

You are learning how decisions get made and how disagreement gets resolved. No course teaches that.

3

Become the biggest source of ideas

For the product you already work on. Then implement them. Shipped ideas make you hard to ignore.

4

Take a good PM class

Mostly for the vocabulary you will be expected to speak fluently in interviews.

Nobody:

Every aspiring PM's LinkedIn headline:

“Passionate about building 0 to 1 products at the intersection of technology, business and design.”

What credibility actually looks like:

Six posts in six months on how the job is really done

Compared with other fields there is remarkably little written about the practice of product management. That gap is your opening. Launch retros, an exec review that went wrong, a reading list, a product you love and how you would fix it.

Part 3

The assignment nobody asked for

A resume is your highlights. An assignment is your thinking. Send it with the resume, before the first round. If it is good, the interview starts on your page, not theirs.

Worked on Groww: India's largest investment platform by NSE active clients, 12.58 million, a 26% share, listed since November 2025. A platform that went from 5 to 12.5 million clients in two years has onboarded a lot of first-timers.

Groww RHP, June 2025 figures.

Second-time investors on Groww
Pre-interview assignment  ·  one page  ·  A. Sharma
Problem

First-time users who buy one mutual fund and never come back. I asked five friends who opened Groww in the last year; three made exactly one purchase.

Why now

12.5M NSE active clients, most of them new to markets. Retention of first-timers is now the whole growth story.

Proposal

A “what happens next” card on the order-success screen: the next SIP date, what the fund did last month in one line, and one tap to set a monthly amount.

Metric

Second purchase within 30 days. Guess: from roughly one in three to one in two for new users.

Risks

I do not know the current second-purchase rate or what onboarding already shows. I would want two weeks of funnel data before committing.

Every question maps back to the five attributes

Seven question types. Five things being scored.

Prepare against the attributes, not against a list of questions.

“Tell me about yourself” has a shape

Ninety seconds. Four beats. Then stop talking.

The common failure is a four-minute chronological walk from school onwards. The interviewer stops listening at ninety seconds. Problem, place, path, why now. Then hand the conversation back.

Product exercises

Five moves, in this order, every time

1

Clarify the objective

“When you say improve, is that revenue, retention or reach?” Thirty seconds that saves nine minutes.

2

Top issues first, then depth

Name the three things that matter, pick one, go deep. Never depth on the first thing you thought of.

3

Show the options you considered

Two or three, briefly. Judgement is only visible against alternatives.

4

Name your own drawbacks

Before they do. The single strongest signal of product sense in the room.

5

“I don't know” is allowed

Followed by how you would find out. Bluffing is the one thing that reliably ends a loop.

∞

Practice is the whole tip

“It takes me three weeks to prepare a good impromptu speech.” Mark Twain. Interview two or three places you can afford to lose before the one you love.

Part 6

You have offers. Some PM jobs suck.

Four kinds, and you can spot every one of them from inside the interview. One question to ask every interviewer: “Walk me through the last thing a PM here shipped, and who decided it.”

1

The PM without any engineers

You own a strategy, someone else owns the roadmap. Every feature is a negotiation with a peer who does not report to you.

2

Engineering is not a core strength

Engineering becomes a service organisation. Good engineers leave, and the ones who stay are not engaged in the product.

3

Nobody knows what a PM is for

You were hired to carry documents between departments that do not talk. Your title says PM. Your job is courier.

4

Not in charge of an actual product

Including the PM who reports into marketing. If nothing ships because of you, it is not the job you were hired for.

The one rule for choosing

Gets PM. Builds good products. Has smart engineers.

All three. A company with two of the three teaches you the wrong habits, and the market prices you on the habits, not the logo.
