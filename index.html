What hiring managers
are looking for
Before you fix your resume, understand what the resume is being measured against.
From hiring, and from being hired

Three lessons worth internalising

  1. Credibility built before you walk through the door is priceless
    The interviewer who spent thirty minutes on your writing is not interviewing you. They are confirming a decision they already made.
  2. PM roles usually require prior PM experience
    The catch-22. You cannot get the job without the experience, and you cannot get the experience without the job. Part 02 is how you break it.
  3. Job hunting makes you sharper
    Applying, researching and interviewing is itself a product education. You come out of the process a better thinker than you went in.
Rethink Systems.
Lesson one, drawn out

What is in front of them the night before

The second conversation is not an evaluation. It is a confirmation. Credibility built before you walk through the door is priceless, and it is built over months, not in the week the opening appears.

Rethink Systems.
Lesson three, drawn out

The search is not dead time. It is the education.

Every company you research is a free product teardown. Every interview is a live case study with a domain expert. Candidates who treat the search as overhead learn nothing from it. Candidates who treat it as practice arrive at the offer already better than they were.

Take a step back

Every interview question is a proxy for one of five attributes

01
Has product sense
Usually makes the right call, at the macro and the micro level
02
Is smart
Structures an unfamiliar problem without being handed a framework
03
Gets things done
Ships. Has a track record of things that actually reached users
04
Is a culture fit
Works the way this specific team works, not the way PMs work in general
05
Has technical skills
Domain expertise plus a real understanding of the technology
Rethink Systems.
Attribute one, defined
Product sense is the ability to usually make the right product decision.
Both macro and micro. Note the word usually. Nobody is scoring you on being right every time.
Rethink Systems.
Attribute five, defined

Technical skills = domain expertise + understanding the technology

What is being tested
Can you explain the basics of the technologies your product depends on?
Not write the code. Explain the system: what calls what, what breaks, what it costs, why a change is a week and not a day.
Why it carries weight
Engineering usually has a large say in the hiring decision
In most PM loops there is an engineering interviewer with real veto power. They are not checking your syntax. They are checking whether working with you will be expensive.
Rethink Systems.
The answer that ends the loop
“I am a business person. Next question, please.”
As a PM, technology is your business. This answer reads as an unwillingness to learn, and it travels back to the panel.

02
Getting real PM experience
before you have the title
How to break the catch-22 without spending two years and twenty lakhs on it.
Before anything else

Be sure you will actually enjoy the job

What people imagine the role is
  • The face of the product
  • Highly compensated, clearly a leader
  • Visionary, future CEO
  • Finally uses the business degree
What most weeks actually contain
  • Responsibility without authority
  • Engineers reassigned mid-project
  • A difficult exec review you did not cause
  • A sales-driven roadmap and angry customers

Talk to at least eight product managers about the good and, more usefully, the bad. If the bad still sounds like a fair trade, continue.

Rethink Systems.
Where should you go to become a PM?

Wrong answer: business school

The “MBA first, PM next” mindset is a deferred life plan. A degree is not an escalator that carries you up. It is a door-knob: it opens a door you still have to walk through on your own legs. Before you commit two years and the fees, confirm your expectations are realistic.

Rethink Systems.
Where should you go to become a PM?

Right answer: your current company

Get hands-on PM experience where you already have context, credibility and access. You are after the skills and the perspective that make people take you seriously when you apply. The observation that makes this work: PMs are busy, and can always use help from a capable colleague.

Rethink Systems.
Four moves, starting Monday

How to do it from where you sit

  1. Approach your PM and offer to help
    Be specific: what you will do, for how long, and what the goal is. A vague offer to help is easy to decline.
  2. Shadow PMs in their core team meetings
    You are learning how decisions get made and how disagreement gets resolved. That is the part no course teaches.
  3. Become the biggest source of ideas for the product you already work on
    Then implement them. Ideas alone make you a commentator. Shipped ideas make you hard to ignore.
  4. Take a good PM class
    Especially useful for picking up the vocabulary you will be expected to speak fluently in interviews.
Rethink Systems.
The objection everyone has
“This is too much work. Is there an easier way?”
Being resourceful under constraint is the job. Treat this as the first test of your suitability. Give it six months, then put the experience at the top of your search.
Rethink Systems.
The credibility play

Write about the practice of product management

Compared with other fields there is remarkably little written about how the job is actually done. That gap is your opening. “I have nothing to write about” is almost never true:

How to manage a launch
How to run an effective exec review
Five mistakes I made and what I learned
An essential reading list for PMs
How to work with sales, engineering, design
A product you love, and how you would improve it

03
The pre-interview
assignment
The highest-leverage thing most candidates never do.
Why bother

A resume is your highlights. An assignment is your thinking.

What a resume can show
Your most featured highlights. Where you worked, what shipped, which numbers moved. Real, but curated, and it says very little about the acumen you hold.
What an assignment shows
How you break down a problem you were not briefed on. Two signals land at once: you cared enough to do unpaid work, and the interviewer can see you think.

More than background, a good product company is hiring a good thinker.

Rethink Systems.
The work does three jobs at once

You are preparing while you are impressing

Company
You learn what they actually do
Not the careers page version. What the product is, who uses it, where the money comes from.
Product
You learn where they are heading
Recent releases tell you the bets. Swiggy Super tells you more about the roadmap than any job description will.
Role
You learn what they are hiring for
Read the LinkedIn profiles of their current PMs. If most came from design, design is the emphasis. Prepare accordingly.
Rethink Systems.
A worked example

Groww has a PM opening. Here is the hour you spend.

  1. Open the app and use it properly
    Groww lets users make investment choices. Go through the flow the way a first-time investor would, not the way an analyst would.
  2. Understand how investing actually happens there
    Onboarding, KYC, discovery, the decision moment, the follow-through after you have put money in.
  3. Map it against real people you know
    Your own investing pattern, and the pattern of the people around you. The gaps show up in the distance between the two.
  4. Pick one gap and write the assignment
    Trust, customer service and expert advice are three places worth digging. Choose one problem statement, not four.
Rethink Systems.
04
The resume
Five sections. Facts do the work, not adjectives.
The one rule underneath all five sections
Impress with facts, not with big words that make you look important.
Because they do not. PMs are judged on actionable points, and the worst thing you can hand a hiring manager is a resume full of non-actionable jargon. The summary drives a go or no-go decision in under a minute: keep it short and real.
Rethink Systems.
Section 01

Objective

Weak
A visionary product management executive with vast strategic experience driving operational efficiencies at Fortune 100 enterprises with C-level visibility.

Every word is doing PR. Nothing is verifiable, so nothing is memorable.

Strong
Five years building enterprise monitoring products for IBM, HP and Applied Materials. Those products generated more than 200 million in revenue.

Same length. Every claim checkable.

Rethink Systems.
Section 01, if you have no PM experience yet

The same rule still applies

Final-year student interested in product management and building user-centric products. Led the college fest committee across three online events with 500+ participants, and drove 20 lakhs in revenue.

You have no PM title. You do have scope, constraint and a number. That is the whole recipe. Enthusiasm is not a qualification, but what you did with your enthusiasm is.

Rethink Systems.
Section 02

Experience

The trap
Writing everything about the product and everything you personally did on it.

Three bullets maximum per role. Last three or four roles only. Always speak to user impact, never to the intermediate changes that produced it.

What each bullet answers
  • What was the product
  • Why was it special, meaning what was the impact
  • What key challenge did you overcome
Led the live class platform at BYJU'S. Drove personalised 1:25 classes to over 100k students a day. Built the strategy for student-to-student and student-to-tutor relationships at scale.
Rethink Systems.
Section 03

Achievements

This section has one job: plant hooks that make the interviewer curious enough to ask. A good hook becomes a conversation you control, in an hour where most things are being asked of you.

With experience
Awarded a 3,000 dollar bounty by Uber for finding bugs in their app.
Without experience
Raised 50 lakhs of funding in college for an event. Or: among the first five teams to build [thing] on campus.

Section 04

Education

Weak
MBA, [school], 2012 to 2016, GPA 3.1
BTech, IIT Roorkee, GPA 8.5

A GPA nobody asked for, doing nothing for you. If the number is not remarkable, it is just a number that invites a question.

Strong
MBA, [school], 2012 to 2016

Same degree, reframed around relevance and evidence of initiative.

Section 05

Skills

Weak
Leadership, communication, collaboration, creativity, listening, UX, strategy, feature definition, forecasting, marketing, ideation, business skills, PowerPoint, MS Project, Agile, research...

A list anyone could claim, so nobody believes any of it.

Strong
Improved UX measures by 25% through more user feedback collection and faster iteration. Launched three products in two years for a 22% revenue gain. Ran four marketing campaigns at 120% ROI.

Skills should always showcase the value you brought.

05
Research, story
and the interview
Walk in knowing the industry, the company, and the person sitting across from you.
Research well before you apply

Three kinds, in this order

01 Industry
Understand the problem the whole industry is trying to solve
Edtech is trying to solve access to education through quality teaching online and strong pedagogy. Learn the different product models inside that, best done by deconstructing BYJU'S, Vedantu and their peers.
02 Company
Understand what this company does and how the product got here
Deconstruct their product. Read the funding coverage, the founder pieces, the YourStory writeups. If you can use the service, do, because first-hand use is where the gaps become obvious.
03 Interviewer
Understand the person who will be asking
Their profile is a synopsis of how the interview will go. This is the step almost nobody does, and it is the one with the highest return.
Rethink Systems.
Worked example: reading an interviewer profile

Four lines on LinkedIn, four things you can predict

What the profile saysWhat you can reasonably infer
Built edtech products, specifically for kidsHeavily design-focused. Expect questions about user empathy and interface decisions.
Handled P&L at EurekaUnderstands business models. Expect commercial framing and unit economics.
Built ML productsComfortable with technology. Expect at least a basic technical probe.
Ran analytics at FlipkartData-led. Expect to be asked how you would measure it.
Rethink Systems.
Turning the inference into preparation

Each background produces a predictable question type

Design-leaning interviewer
“How would you design a bathroom for someone who is blind?”
Practise reasoning from the user's constraint outward, not from the feature inward.
Business-leaning interviewer
“Should Udemy move into live learning?”
Practise taking a position, then defending it against the strongest counter-argument.
Analytics-leaning interviewer
“How many Teslas are sold in New York in a day?”
Practise estimation out loud. The number is irrelevant. The structure is the answer.
Rethink Systems.
What most PM and APM loops contain

Six stages, rarely in a different order

  1. Tell me about yourself
    Sell the story. This is the only question you can fully prepare, and most people waste it.
  2. What is product management, and why are you a fit for it?
  3. How would you improve our product, or a product of your choice?
  4. An assignment based on the business
  5. A technical round, sometimes
  6. Compensation
Rethink Systems.
Stage one, done properly
The interviewer wants to interview you if they think they will take something away from the hour.
Your story is the thing that makes that likely. Build it deliberately.
Rethink Systems.
A good story answers three questions

Problem, place, path

  1. Why did this problem interest you?
    Say it at the scale you actually think at. Solving payments for 300 million students. Solving learning for a 15 billion dollar economy. Then give it a relatable handle: “Stripe for India”.
  2. Why does the place you worked excite you?
    Describe the growth or the business model, not the brand. “Shopify for India, at this stage of growth, with this model.”
  3. Why does your path make sense, and why is this switch next?
    If you have moved quickly between jobs, own it with a clear through-line. A short Loom explaining your story works as a visual resume and almost nobody sends one.

Inside the interview

Every question falls into one of seven categories

CategoryTypically asked as
Product senseYour favourite product and why. Compare Facebook and Twitter.
Product management basicsWhat does a PM do. What are the hard parts. How do you prioritise features.
ProcessHow would you manage a complex launch. How do you handle a difficult team member.
Past projects and productsWhy did you choose X over Y. How did you handle that failure.
Brain teasers and problem solvingEstimation and structured reasoning under time pressure.
Domain expertiseHow is company X positioned against Y. Name four pain points in our vertical.
Understanding of technologyExplain how the thing you built actually worked.

On product sense questions

There is a line, and most answers never cross it

You do not need to be profound for the whole hour. You need it once. Pick the product you know most deeply and find the one observation about it that the interviewer has not heard before.
